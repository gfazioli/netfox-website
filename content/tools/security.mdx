# Security

Netfox doesn't just tell you *who's* on your network — it also tells you *what looks wrong*. The Security tool runs a quick check on every device it can reach, flags anything risky in plain English, and lets you fix it from the device that has the problem.

## What gets checked

For every device on your home network, Netfox can probe a short list of well-known ports — the ones that matter for home security:

| Category | Looking for |
|---|---|
| Remote access | SSH, Telnet, RDP, VNC |
| File sharing | FTP, SMB, AFP, NFS |
| Web & IoT control | HTTP, HTTPS, MQTT, Home Assistant |
| Discovery | NetBIOS, mDNS, SSDP |
| Streaming & media | AirPlay, Plex, Transmission |
| Developer & database | MySQL, PostgreSQL, Redis, MongoDB, and common dev servers (Node/Next.js, Vite, Angular, Django, Jupyter) |

The dev-server ports are there so you can catch a framework server you left running and reachable across your LAN. Unlike the database ports, they don't raise a risk finding on their own — a dev server turning up won't push a device into a higher risk badge. And when a service lives on a port the catalog doesn't know, you can add it yourself — see [Custom ports](#custom-ports) below.

Each port comes back as one of three states:

- **Open** — the service is actively listening
- **Closed** — nothing's listening
- **Filtered** — a firewall is dropping the probe

Probes are quick TCP connections (no packet sniffing, no root access). They only target devices on your local network — Netfox refuses to probe anything outside your LAN, even if you ask.

## Risk badges

Once a device has been probed, it earns a coloured badge:

- 🟢 **Low risk** — nothing obviously dangerous open
- 🟡 **Medium risk** — at least one finding worth knowing about (an HTTP-only admin page, a slightly outdated service)
- 🔴 **High risk** — something you should fix soon (Telnet wide open, a database port reachable from the LAN, a remote-access service without TLS)

Click the badge to open the **Risk Inspector** — a plain-English explanation of every finding, including which port it's on and what to do about it. No security jargon.

## Running a scan

![Netfox Security](/screenshot-security.png)

Open the **Security** tab in the left sidebar (or press <Kbd>⌘</Kbd> <Kbd>4</Kbd>). You'll see a dashboard summarising the network's posture:

- Header counts ("3 devices need your attention")
- Severity pills (high / medium / low / untested)
- One section per severity, with the affected devices

The blue **Scan All Devices** button kicks off probes across every untested device in parallel. The Security tool is rate-limited under the hood, so it can't flood your router. While a scan is running, the button reads "Scanning…" and devices migrate out of "Untested" into their real severity bucket as their results come in.

Once everything has been scanned at least once, the button flips to **Rescan All Devices** — port state ages (services start, firewalls get reconfigured), and re-running the sweep picks up anything that's changed.

You can also probe a single device:

- Click **Probe** next to any untested row
- Or right-click any device in Overview → **Probe Ports**
- Or press <Kbd>⌘</Kbd> <Kbd>⇧</Kbd> <Kbd>P</Kbd> with a device selected

## Finding the affected port

Click any risk row in the Security dashboard to jump straight to that device's **Ports** tab in Overview. Every port that triggered a finding has its risk symbol painted next to it — you can spot the troublesome port at a glance instead of cross-referencing the Risk Inspector.

## Scheduled scans

If you'd rather Netfox keep an eye on your network continuously, open **Settings → Security** and flip **Auto-probe ports on a schedule** on. Pick an interval from 1 hour to 1 week (default: every 6 hours). Netfox will re-scan every scannable device on that cadence — same rate limits, same LAN-only check, no extra setup.

![Settings Security tab with auto-probe schedule](/screenshot-settings-security.png)

The toggle is off by default. The manual **Scan All Devices** button covers the common case ("did anything change?"); the schedule is for users who want continuous coverage without having to remember to click.

## Custom ports

The built-in catalog covers the ports a home network is most likely to care about — but your network might not be everyone's network. A dev server pinned to `:9281`, an admin panel on `:10443`, a self-hosted service on whatever port you gave it: add them in **Settings → Security → Custom ports** and every scan checks them, on top of the catalog.

Entries are separated by commas or new lines, each a port number with an optional label after `=`:

```
9281 = Next.js dev
10443 = Admin UI
8081
```

A few things the list does for you:

- **Your labels show up everywhere** — the device detail panel, the Services view and alerts all call the port by the name you gave it, instead of a generic "Port 9281"
- **Custom ports get their own section** in the device detail panel, so your additions stay visually distinct from the curated catalog
- **Typos can't break anything** — entries that aren't valid port numbers are simply ignored, and a live caption under the field shows exactly which ports registered
- **No restart needed** — changes apply from the next scan, manual or scheduled

<Callout type="info">
Custom ports can only *add* checks. If you list a port that's already in the built-in catalog, the catalog's name wins — so SSH stays "SSH" no matter what you call it.
</Callout>

## Privacy

Everything stays on your Mac:

- Probes only fire against devices on your own LAN (verified twice — once by the UI, once by the engine).
- No data leaves your computer. Netfox doesn't ship findings to a cloud service.
- **Demo Mode** (<Kbd>⌘</Kbd> <Kbd>⇧</Kbd> <Kbd>D</Kbd>) masks device names, MAC addresses, IPv6, and Wi-Fi SSIDs so you can take screenshots without exposing your setup. Findings stay visible — only the identifying labels around them are masked. See [Settings → Privacy](/docs/settings#privacy) for the full mask reference.
- Probe history lives in a local SQLite store alongside the device history. **Settings → Privacy** has an *Erase port history* button if you ever want to wipe the slate.

## Why these checks, not others

The home-LAN focus is deliberate. Netfox's checks target the things that actually matter on a typical home network:

- An IoT plug exposing its config page over plain HTTP
- A NAS with SMB1 still enabled
- A router admin panel reachable via Telnet
- A development database port (3306, 5432) accidentally bound to all interfaces

Full pentest tooling (raw-socket scanning, OS fingerprinting via TCP/IP quirks, packet capture for topology) requires root access on macOS and lives outside the sandbox Netfox runs in. That work is planned for a future privileged-helper era — for now, the unprivileged checks above already catch the vast majority of home-network problems.
