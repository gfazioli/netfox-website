# Privacy policy

_Last updated: 5 October 2026_

The short version: everything Netfox learns about your network stays on your Mac. There is no account, no telemetry and no usage analytics. A few requests do leave your network, and each one is listed below, with the switch that stops it where there is one.

## The Netfox app

### What leaves your network

- **Your public IP address.** To show the face your network presents to the internet, Netfox asks a public-IP lookup service over HTTPS at launch and when you refresh the Overview. On by default; **Settings → Privacy → Detect public IP** turns it off.
- **The link check.** While the Overview's link panel is on screen, Netfox pings a public DNS server (1.1.1.1) every 15 seconds to measure the route to the internet. Only ping packets travel. The same switch turns it off.
- **Update checks.** The app asks this website whether a new version exists, carrying the app's version. The scheduled check runs only after you allow it (the update framework asks you once, on the second launch), and *Check for updates on launch* is off by default; see [Settings → Updates](/docs/settings#updates). A new version's notes come from this website and its download from GitHub.
- **The support window.** It opens at launch unless you turn it off (see [Settings → General](/docs/settings#support-window)), and in About. It shows a sponsor's picture, which is loaded from GitHub.

Nothing else leaves your network. Netfox has no analytics, no crash reporter and no remote configuration, and its device-vendor database is built into the app rather than looked up online.

### What stays on your local network

Discovery, pings to your devices, port checks, the service details it reads and the Web Viewer all talk only to devices on networks your Mac is attached to. The [Devices](/docs/tools/devices) and [Security](/docs/tools/security) pages describe each of them.

**Location permission.** macOS hides Wi-Fi network names from apps without it. Netfox asks for it only to read the networks around you, and never reads your coordinates.

### What it stores on your Mac

Your devices and the names and tags you give them, each device's timeline (30 days), alerts (90 days), port results (30 days), latency samples (7 days) and Wi-Fi samples, including the networks' hardware addresses (7 days). They stay on your Mac. [Settings → Privacy](/docs/settings#storage) clears them; the Wi-Fi samples age out on their own after 7 days. The optional metrics endpoint is off by default, answers only on this Mac unless you open it to your network, and then requires a token; see [Integrations](/docs/integrations).

## This website

- **No cookies, no cross-site tracking.** The site sets no cookies of its own and does not follow you across other sites.
- **Visit statistics.** Visits are counted by privacy-friendly analytics that use no cookies and build no profile of you: pages viewed, the referring site, country, and browser or device type, in aggregate. Visits are grouped by a hash of the request that is discarded after 24 hours.
- **Server logs.** The hosting provider that serves these pages, and the app's update checks, processes your IP address and the details of each request to deliver the site and keep it secure; the request logs we can see are kept for one day.
- **Your browser's local storage** remembers, in your browser only, that you closed the release banner. None of it is sent to us.
- **Email.** If you write to us, we use your address and message only to answer you, and keep them no longer than the conversation needs.
- **Pictures from other services.** The home page shows launch badges loaded from Product Hunt and Fazier, and the sponsor pictures at the bottom of every page are loaded from GitHub. Those services therefore see your IP address. Downloads also come from GitHub.
- **Links.** GitHub Sponsors and donations through Stripe are on their own services. If you use them, their own privacy policies apply.
