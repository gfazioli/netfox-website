Netfox 0.1.0

Release notes

The first public build of Netfox. A native macOS app that shows you every device on your home network, when each one joined, and when something new shows up — at a glance.

What you'll see on first launch

  • Live device list. Every device on your network in one window — Mac, iPhone, smart TV, printer, the lot. Refreshed continuously, no manual scanning needed.
  • Your Mac, on top. The Mac running Netfox is auto-detected, pinned to the top of the list, with the correct icon and a label saying whether it's on Wi-Fi or Ethernet.
  • Per-device timeline. Click any device to see when it first appeared on your network, when it went offline, and when it came back. No more "is the iPad actually on?" guessing.

Alerts that matter

  • New device joined. A notification fires when something Netfox has never seen before connects to your network — useful for spotting a visitor's phone or a smart bulb you forgot you owned.
  • Known device returned. Optional alert when a device that's been offline for a while comes back. Great for "the laptop is home from the trip" — quiet about the phone that leaves every morning and returns every evening. Off by default; turn it on in Settings → Alerts and set the absence threshold in days.
  • Persistent log. Every alert lands in the in-app history (View → Alert History) so you can scroll back through what happened, even if you missed the notification.

Make it yours

  • Manual tagging. Right-click any device → Edit tag… → give it a real name and an icon. Replaces the cryptic vendor names ("Espressif Inc.", "Hon Hai Precision") that mean nothing without context.
  • Theme picker. System / Light / Dark — under Settings → General.
  • Open at login. One toggle in Settings → General flips the system setting directly. No login items list to dig through.
  • Advanced toggles. Two offline-detection thresholds for users who want to tune how fast the app marks a device as gone.

Native, signed, no account

  • Real macOS app — proper window, system materials, dark/light mode follows the system, keyboard shortcuts everywhere (⌘R refresh, ⌘, Settings, ⌘W close).
  • Signed with Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple. First launch goes straight through without warnings.
  • Universal binary — runs natively on Apple Silicon and Intel Macs.
  • No account, no cloud, no telemetry. Everything stays on your Mac.

Compatibility

  • macOS 15 (Sequoia) or newer.

Known limitations (first release)

  • No auto-update yet. The next version will need to be downloaded manually from netfox.app until the auto-update channel is wired.
  • Router fingerprinting is imperfect. Some routers and IoT devices may show up as "Unknown" or with a generic vendor name. Tag them manually in the meantime.
  • Brief flicker on power-managed devices. iPads in Low Power Mode and similar power-conscious hardware may blink offline/online for a second or two; detection is being tuned for the next build.
