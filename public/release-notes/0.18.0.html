Netfox 0.18.0

Release notes

One question this release answers without a terminal, a router admin page or a speed test: is my connection the problem, or is it something past my door?

The link, in plain English

The Overview has a new panel. It reads the physical layer of this Mac's own connection — the part underneath the IP address, where the cable and the radio actually live — and says what it finds.

What the cable negotiated, against what it could have

  • A port settling for less than it can do is named. A 10-gigabit port that negotiated 1 Gb/s is not broken, and nothing in macOS tells you it happened. Netfox says so, and says the two usual reasons: the cable, or the switch at the other end. Cat 5e runs 1 Gb/s; 10 needs Cat 6a and a switch that speaks it.
  • Full duplex and flow control, where they are a verdict. They are shown coloured because there is a right answer. Auto-negotiation is shown plainly, because a hand-pinned link is unusual rather than wrong.

What the radio is doing

  • The generation, the rate, and the room you have. Wi-Fi 7 at 1.2 Gb/s means little on its own; "39 dB of headroom — signal −58 dBm against −97 dBm of noise" means you have room to spare.
  • The band, the width and the channel — only when there is one. A radio that is switched off has no band, and a screen that fills those in with "Unknown" has invented two facts out of an absence.
  • Whether the rate is holding. The panel takes two samples and tells you what changed between them: steady, renegotiated up, renegotiated down. This is not decoration — during development the radio moved from 648 Mb/s to 1.2 Gb/s between two samples two seconds apart, on the same network, in the same room. A single reading calls that a property of your link. It is not one.

Whether anything is being dropped

Two separate claims, because neither implies the other:

  • Right now — what crossed the link in the last two seconds, and whether any of it faulted.
  • Since boot — the same question over the machine's uptime.

A link that has been clean for weeks can start dropping frames this second, and one that dropped a handful during a cable swap last month is clean now. And a link nothing crossed gets no clean bill of health: it has not been shown to be fine, it has not been shown anything.

And how far away the delay starts

  • The first hop, and everything past it. "First hop 2.9 ms, then 12 ms more past the router" splits the question in two: your own link, and the internet beyond it. That split is the whole point — it is the difference between a problem you can fix and one you can only wait out.
  • A router that ignores pings is not a broken router. Many drop them by policy. Netfox says it cannot time the first hop rather than reporting a fault.
  • Packet loss outranks speed. A flaky cable is quick right up until the packet that vanishes.
  • The first hop never leaves your network. The comparison past the router uses a public resolver, and only if you have left Netfox's internet access on — the same switch that controls the public-IP lookup. Turn it off and the panel reports the first hop alone rather than inventing a comparison.

The Overview fits on one screen

Five panels and a scrollbar, on the screen whose whole job is to be readable at a glance. The last one was always below the fold on a large window, and the space it needed was being spent above it.

  • The sponsor buttons moved into the corner beside the logo instead of taking a row of their own.
  • The banner that counted devices needing attention became the first card of the summary, with its Smart Scan button. It and the risk card had been printing the same number twice, in two sizes, a hundred points apart.
  • The network card and the new link card sit side by side when the window is wide enough, and stack when it is not. They are the same story told logically and physically.

Fixes

  • The What's New panel is translated. It shipped in English inside six otherwise-translated languages, along with its menu item.
  • Netfox no longer advertises what it has already built. Two lists inside the app were still calling shipped features "coming".
  • Band and Channel Width read correctly in Italian, Spanish, French and Portuguese. Both showed an "Unknown" written for a different word, so the adjective disagreed with the label beside it. English inflects neither, so the screen looked finished in every language.
  • A Wi-Fi generation newer than this build knows is no longer reported as "not connected". The next generation of hardware would have shown a live link labelled disconnected.

Under the hood

  • The panel refuses to draw a container for an absence. No section header over an empty section, no pills for a radio that is not associated, no green seal over a sample where nothing moved. Each of those was in a draft and each was caught by rendering the panel rather than by reading it.
  • Every latency figure is a minimum, never an average. Measured across five bursts: the minimum moved by under a millisecond on both a router and a host on the internet, while the average moved by more than a hundred on both — one delayed reply drags a mean of five and moves a minimum not at all. The first explanation for that was wrong and four more measurements killed it.
  • Counters are read at 64 bits. The 32-bit form wraps, and a wrapped counter turns a healthy link into an astronomical number on a screen. A counter that goes backwards is refused rather than subtracted.
  • The threshold for a slow first hop is a named constant with its calibration beside it — about eight times the floor measured on one machine, which is "generous enough not to cry wolf" rather than "the boundary of healthy".
  • Nothing new is asked of you. The physical link, the radio and the counters are all read without a privilege, a permission prompt or a helper. The only traffic this feature generates is the latency measurement, and that is behind the switch you already have.
  • Six languages, in step. 858/858 in Italian, French, German, Spanish, Portuguese and Dutch, plus 89/89 in the framework catalog.
  • 774 tests, green.

Install

  1. Download Netfox-0.18.0.dmg
  2. Open the DMG and drag Netfox into Applications
  3. Launch normally — the app is signed with Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple

Requirements

macOS 15.6 or later.
